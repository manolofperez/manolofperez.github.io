The lab is based at the Real Jardín Botánico in Madrid, a living museum with more than 265 years of history and a research centre of the Spanish National Research Council (CSIC). The garden's mission is to understand and conserve the biodiversity of plants, fungi and other organisms, and to train people to do the same at every level of education. We add a computational layer to that mission.

Our work sits on complex diversification processes: the recent, messy, incompletely sorted parts of the tree of life where species boundaries blur and demography leaves its clearest marks. To get at them we develop Bayesian and machine-learning methods, in particular neural networks trained on simulated data, for species delimitation, phylogeography, population genetics, and phylogenetic and diversification analyses.

Being inside a botanical garden shapes the questions. Pleistocene refugia in Neotropical cacti, admixture in the Canarian flora and the conservation of threatened endemics sit next to projects on Amazonian fishes, primate diversification and the transmission dynamics of pathogens such as HIV and the malaria vectors of the Anopheles gambiae complex. The same simulators and networks serve all of them.

Research lines

Four threads, one toolbox. Each line pairs a biological question with the simulation-based inference needed to answer it.

HIV · primates · birth–death models

Deep learning from phylogenies

Tree representations and neural architectures that let a network read a phylogeny directly, for model selection and parameter estimation in phylodynamics (BD, BDEI, BDSS) and macroevolutionary diversification (BiSSE and relatives). PhyloCNN is the current flagship.

Pilosocereus · Kleinia · Euphorbia · Hoplias

Species delimitation & phylogeography of recent radiations

Where coalescent methods and taxonomy disagree, we simulate under explicit demographic models and let CNNs or ABC decide: interglacial microrefugia in cactus complexes, island admixture in Macaronesia, and species complexes in the gray zone of speciation.

Osteoglossiformes · Characiformes · Loricariidae

Genomic & cytogenomic resources for non-model species

Combining population genomics with comparative cytogenetics in Neotropical fishes: karyotype turnover, neo-sex chromosomes and chromosomal rearrangements as drivers of divergence in arowanas, arapaima, trahiras and armored catfish.

Anopheles · steppe biota · threatened endemics

Climate, conservation & generative models

Species distribution modelling and remote-sensing data to assess climate-change impacts on endangered species, and continental-scale reconstructions of responses to Quaternary change. The newest branch applies generative models to evolutionary inference in malaria vectors, and asks how AI should be used in science at all.

    Principal investigator

    Manolo Fernandez Perez

    Manolo is a researcher at the Real Jardín Botánico in Madrid (CSIC). He trained as an evolutionary geneticist, earning his PhD at the Federal University of São Carlos in Brazil with work on the phylogeography of cacti from dry Neotropical environments, and has since moved steadily toward the computational side of the field.

    His research develops Bayesian and machine-learning methods for population genetics, species delimitation, biogeography, and phylogenetic and diversification analyses, applied to datasets that range from macroevolution to epidemiology. He contributes to the stdpopsim consortium and teaches machine learning for population genetics internationally.

    Before Madrid he held positions at Imperial College London (I-X, AI in science), the Muséum national d'Histoire naturelle in Paris, and the Fish Cytogenetics Lab at UFSCar.
